# 07 — Academia, Teaching & Research APPEs

Back to [index](../README.md).

If there's any chance you want to teach, do this rotation. Academia is the one career path
where a P4 elective maps almost exactly onto the day-to-day job, and hiring committees
notice when a candidate has already stood in front of a classroom.

---

## The two flavors of academic APPE

### 1. Teaching / academic APPE
Hosted at a college of pharmacy, usually precepted by a faculty member. Typical activities:

- Delivering lectures or small-group sessions in a didactic course
- Facilitating recitation, lab, or case-based discussions
- Writing and validating exam questions
- Grading and giving structured feedback
- Developing a **teaching philosophy statement** — the artifact you'll need for any faculty
  application
- Sitting in on curriculum committee or assessment work
- Peer teaching and near-peer mentoring of P1–P3 students

### 2. Clinical academia APPE
The hybrid: you follow a clinical faculty member through both their patient care service and
their teaching load. You see how the job actually splits — rounding in the morning, lecturing
in the afternoon, precepting throughout. AACP has programmed specifically on this model:
[From Clinic to Classroom: Perspectives on Clinical Academia APPE Rotations](https://www.aacp.org/event/clinic-classroom-perspectives-clinical-academia-appe-rotations).

This is the more honest preview of a clinical faculty career, and generally the more useful
of the two if you're deciding whether academia is for you.

  Pharmaceutical Education* are where academic pharmacy talks to itself

## How to get a teaching APPE at your own school

Usually the easiest competitive rotation to land, because the preceptors are people you
already know.

1. **Identify the faculty member** whose course or clinical service you'd want to work in.
   Pick someone whose subject you actually did well in — they'll remember you.
2. **Ask in your P3 year**, well before scheduling. Teaching APPEs are often informal and
   don't appear in the site list until someone asks for one.
3. **Bring a proposal**, not a request: which course, which weeks, what you'd deliver.
4. **Ask whether your school has a Teaching Certificate program** — many do, some open to P4
   students, most aimed at residents. Either way, knowing it exists helps you plan.

If your school won't host one, nearby colleges of pharmacy sometimes will. In the Northeast
that's a long list — see [09 — Northeast & Mid-Atlantic Sites](09-northeast-mid-atlantic-sites.md).

## Research APPEs

Distinct from teaching, and valuable for a different reason: a research rotation can produce
a **poster or a manuscript**, which is durable CV material.

**Where to find them:**

| Setting | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| **College of pharmacy labs** | Pharmaceutics, pharmacology, pharmacoepidemiology, health services research, social & administrative sciences |
| **Academic medical center** | Clinical research led by pharmacy departments; retrospective chart reviews; quality improvement projects |
| **Investigational Drug Service (IDS)** | Trial drug handling, randomization, protocol compliance — see [04](04-industry-and-regulatory.md) |
| **NIH Clinical Center** | Its APPE includes a pharmacokinetics / clinical research / clinical pharmacology track — see [03](03-federal-government.md) |
| **NCI, St. Jude** | Oncology research settings; [St. Jude APPE](https://www.stjude.org/education-training/clinical-training/clinical-residencies/pharmacy-residency-programs/advanced-pharmacy-practice-experience-rotations.html) — apply the year prior, early in P3 |
| **CROs** | IQVIA, Parexel, Syneos — clinical operations and real-world evidence |
| **Pharmacoepidemiology groups** | Often housed in schools of public health; large-claims-database work |

**Set expectations honestly:** a 4–6 week block is not enough to run a study. What it *is*
enough for is a literature review, a data analysis on an existing dataset, a poster abstract,
or a defined piece of a larger project. Agree with your preceptor **in week one** on a
concrete deliverable, and ask directly whether it can become an abstract for a national
meeting.

## Longitudinal APPEs

Some schools offer **longitudinal** rotations — one day a week over several months rather
than a full-time block. These suit research and teaching especially well, since both benefit
from sustained time. Examples of programs that publish this structure:
[University at Buffalo longitudinal rotations](https://pharmacy.buffalo.edu/academics/pharmd/experiential-education/advanced-pharmacy-practice-experiences--appe-/longitudinal-rotations.html) ·
[University of Minnesota longitudinal APPEs](https://www.pharmacy.umn.edu/doctor-pharmacy/longitudinal-appes).

Ask your OEE whether yours does. If it does and nobody uses it, that's an opening.

## The academic career pipeline

Worth understanding now, because it changes which APPEs matter:

```
P4 teaching APPE
   → PGY1 residency
      → PGY2 (specialty) + Teaching Certificate
         → clinical faculty position
```

or, for the research track:

```
P4 research APPE
   → PGY1/PGY2, or a fellowship (2 yr, research-focused)
      → tenure-track or research faculty
```

Two things make the difference at the faculty-hiring stage: **a teaching portfolio** (your
philosophy statement, sample lecture, evaluations) and **scholarship** (posters, papers).
Both start in the P4 year. A teaching APPE plus a research APPE is the strongest two-block
combination for this path.
