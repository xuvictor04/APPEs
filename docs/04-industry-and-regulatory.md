## Named industry APPE programs

### Bristol Myers Squibb — APPE Program ✅ 🔴 open now
**Window:** **September 1–19**. **Letter of recommendation due October 3.** Late materials
are not accepted.
**Format:** **virtual or hybrid**, based on student and preceptor preferences — you do not
need to relocate to New Jersey for this rotation.
**Duration:** 4–6 weeks
**Email:** student.rotations@bms.com
**Link:** [BMS Advanced Degree Programs — APPE](https://careers.bms.com/advanced-degree-programs/)

**Functions offered:** Medical Affairs, Clinical Development, HEOR, Field Medical, Marketing,
Policy and Advocacy, Pharmacovigilance/Epidemiology, Regulatory Affairs, and others. That
breadth is unusual — most industry APPEs are single-function.

**Materials:** CV, **one-page letter of intent**, **one letter of recommendation**, and the
online application.

**Important:** the university contract is **initiated only after acceptance**. A missing
affiliation agreement is not a reason to skip applying — apply first, contract second.

> The highest-value deadline on the calendar today. If industry is your direction, this is
> this week's priority, and the virtual/hybrid format removes the usual logistics objection.

### Cencora (formerly AmerisourceBergen) — APPE Program ✅
**Status:** application-based, **competitive** — explicitly *not* an informal rotation you can
set up by cold-emailing.
**Timing:** annual competitive selection; **timing varies by participating school**.
**Link:** Cencora Pharmacy Education / Cencora APPE Program (via
[cencora.com](https://www.cencora.com/))

**Program:** an APPE in **wholesale drug distribution and supply chain management**. The
annual cohort completes training that culminates with in-person programming at Cencora
headquarters (Conshohocken, PA — inside your region).

**How to apply:** ask your experiential office whether your school is a **participating
university**, then request the current application, the internal nomination process, and the
deadline. Cencora does **not** publish a single national student application form or a
universal deadline on its public pages.

**Why it's worth a look:** drug distribution and supply chain is a large pharmacist employment
sector that almost no student rotates through, and it's directly relevant to drug shortages,
340B, and health-system contracting.

### Boehringer Ingelheim — PharmD Advanced Pharmacy Practice Experiences Program
**Location:** Ridgefield, CT (US headquarters)
**Link:** [PharmD APPE program](https://www.boehringer-ingelheim.com/us/careers/discover-our-career-areas/pharmd-advanced-pharmacy-practice-experiences-program) ·
[Pharmacy rotations](https://www.boehringer-ingelheim.com/us/careers/discover-our-careers/pharmacy-rotations) ·
[Students at BI](https://careers.boehringer-ingelheim.com/students/learn-us)

**Key constraint:** BI hosts students through **contractual agreements with select pharmacy
schools**. Check whether yours is on the list *before* you build an application around it.
If it isn't, your OEE would need to initiate an agreement — a long process.

BI also runs an **Emerging Scientist Talent Program** and a Management Rotation Program;
different tracks, worth knowing they exist.

### Genentech / Roche

**Link:** [careers.gene.com/us/en/students-rotations](https://careers.gene.com/us/en/students-rotations)

Flagged as an explicit correction in the July 2026 edition. **This is not a PharmD APPE.**

included it as a pharmacy-student option. Don't spend time on it.

## What the functional areas actually are

Useful for writing a letter of intent that doesn't sound generic.

| Function | What a pharmacist does there |
| --- | --- |
| **Medical Affairs** | Scientific exchange with clinicians; the bridge between R&D and practice |
| **Medical Information** | Answers unsolicited clinical questions; writes standard response documents |
| **MSL (field medical)** | Field-based scientific liaison to KOLs; heavy travel, high autonomy |
| **Regulatory Affairs** | Submissions (IND, NDA, BLA), labeling, agency interaction |
| **Clinical Development** | Protocol design, trial execution, data interpretation |
| **Pharmacovigilance / Drug Safety** | Adverse event assessment, signal detection, REMS |
| **HEOR** | Cost-effectiveness, value dossiers, payer evidence |
| **Medical Writing** | Protocols, clinical study reports, manuscripts, regulatory documents |
| **Commercial / Marketing** | Brand strategy, launch planning, market access |
| **Market Access / Payer** | Formulary strategy, contracting, AMCP dossiers |

## Tactics for landing an industry rotation

1. **Deadlines are early and hard.** BMS's window is 19 days long. Missing it costs a year.
2. **Check the school-agreement constraint first.** BI and several others only take students
   from partner schools. This is the most common silent disqualifier.
3. **Your letter of intent must name a function.** "I'm interested in industry" reads as
   unserious. "I want to build toward regulatory affairs, and BMS's global regulatory group
   is where I'd learn submission strategy" reads as a candidate.
4. **Join IPhO and show up.** Chapter leadership and IPhO's industry rounds are the cheapest
   networking available to a student.
5. **A local CRO or an IDS rotation is a real industry credential** if the big names don't
   come through. Don't treat them as consolation prizes — they teach the same vocabulary.
6. **LinkedIn works here** in a way it doesn't for hospital rotations. Alumni from your school
   at target companies are your best route to a preceptor who isn't advertising.
