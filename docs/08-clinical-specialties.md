# 08 — Clinical Specialty APPEs

Back to [index](../README.md).

Your electives here are the ones residency program directors read most closely. These are
usually arranged through your school's site network rather than by national application —
so the work is in *choosing* well and in asking your OEE early for the specific sites you want.

## Choosing well

**Match the site to the goal.**
- Chasing a competitive PGY1? Weight toward **academic medical centers with residency
  programs**. RPDs read your preceptor list, and a name they recognize helps.
- Chasing a specific PGY2 (ID, critical care, onc)? Get **at least two blocks** in that area,
  ideally at different institutions so you have two independent letters.
- Not sure yet? Deliberately spread across settings. The P4 year is the last cheap chance to
  rule things out.

**Rotate where you might apply.** An APPE at a hospital with a residency is a months-long
interview. Many programs preferentially match students who rotated there.

**Sequence for letters.** PhORCAS opens in the fall of P4. If you need a letter from a
clinical preceptor, that rotation must happen **before roughly November**. Ask your OEE to
front-load one strong clinical block if residency is the plan.

**Don't stack the two hardest blocks back-to-back.** Critical care immediately after
transplant will flatten you, and the second preceptor sees a tired student.

## Getting a specialty rotation that isn't in your school's list

1. **Ask your OEE first** — the site may already be affiliated and just not advertised.
2. **Find the specific preceptor**, not the department. Board-certified specialists are
   listed on hospital sites, in ASHP residency directories, and on their residency program's
   preceptor page.
3. **Email them directly** with a short, specific request. Template in
   [11 — Application Materials](11-application-materials.md).
4. **Bring a syllabus.** ASHP's free
   [preceptor toolkit templates](https://www.ashp.org/pharmacy-practice/resource-centers/preceptor-toolkit)
   remove the main objection — that building a rotation is too much work.
5. **Give your OEE 4–6 months** for a new affiliation agreement.

## A note on rotation load

A 4–6 week clinical block at an academic medical center is a real job: pre-rounding, topic
discussions, a journal club, a formal presentation, and often a project. Two things help
more than anything else:

- **Ask for the syllabus and expectations a week before you start.** Most preceptors will
  send them, and almost no students ask.
- **Keep a running log of every intervention you make.** You will need concrete examples for
  residency interviews, and you will not remember them in December.
