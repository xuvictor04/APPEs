# 10 — Non-Traditional & Specialty Electives

Back to [index](../README.md).

Rotations most students never think to ask for. Low competition, high differentiation — and
several of them are the best answer to "tell me about a rotation that changed how you think."

---

## Toxicology & poison control

Among the best non-traditional rotations available: real clinical reasoning, pharmacist-led
by default, and genuinely memorable.

**Find your regional center:**
🌐 [HRSA Poison Help — Find a Poison Center](https://poisonhelp.hrsa.gov/poison-centers/find-poison-center) ·
[Drugs.com poison control directory](https://www.drugs.com/poison-control.html)

**Northeast centers with documented student rotations:**
- 🌐 [NYC Poison Control Center](https://www.nyc.gov/site/doh/about/employment/toxicology-rotation.page) — Lower Manhattan; 2–4 week didactic electives in medical toxicology
- [NYU Langone Division of Medical Toxicology](https://med.nyu.edu/emergency/education/visiting-resident-medical-toxicology-rotation) — faculty mentor pharmacy students in partnership with the NYC PCC
- [Poison Control Center at CHOP](https://www.chop.edu/centers-programs/poison-control-center/resources-for-professionals) — Philadelphia; a regular toxicology training site for pharmacy students
- [Maryland Poison Center](https://www.mdpoison.com/) — a service of the University of Maryland School of Pharmacy since 1972; also runs a [clinical toxicology fellowship](https://www.pharmacy.umaryland.edu/residentsfellows/programs/toxicology/)
- 🌐 [UConn Poison Control — clinical student rotators](https://health.uconn.edu/poison-control/health-care-providers/clinical-student-rotators/)
- New Jersey Poison Information & Education System (Rutgers NJMS) — Newark
- National Capital Poison Center — Washington, DC

## Pharmacy informatics

Where pharmacy meets the EHR. Growing fast, and a real career track.

**What you'd do:** order-set and clinical decision support design, alert fatigue reduction,
automated dispensing cabinet management, barcode medication administration, EHR build and
optimization (Epic Willow, Cerner), analytics and reporting.

**Where:** almost any large health system's pharmacy informatics team; the
[NIH Clinical Center](https://www.cc.nih.gov/training/students/clinical_electives) lists
pharmacy informatics as one of its APPE tracks.

**Free syllabus to hand a preceptor:**
[ASHP Informatics APPE Student Rotation (PDF)](https://www.ashp.org/-/media/assets/pharmacy-practice/resource-centers/preceptor-toolkit/sicp-informatics-appe-student-rotation-updated.pdf)

Related: **HIMSS** and ASHP's Section of Pharmacy Informatics and Technology.

## Medication safety

**What you'd do:** root cause analysis, FMEA, medication error reporting and trending, gap
analyses against ISMP Targeted Medication Safety Best Practices, high-alert medication policy.

**Where:** every large health system has a medication safety officer; ISMP itself (Horsham, PA)
and ECRI (Plymouth Meeting, PA) are worth a direct inquiry.

**Templates and models:**
[ASHP medication safety rotation (PDF)](https://www.ashp.org/-/media/assets/pharmacy-practice/resource-centers/preceptor-toolkit/sicp-med-safety-appe-student-rotation-updated.pdf) ·
[Saint Luke's Medication Safety APPE](https://www.saintlukeskc.org/medication-safety-appe) ·
[ISMP](https://www.ismp.org/)

A common student project: read every ISMP Medication Safety Alert published during your
rotation and complete a gap analysis against the current quarterly action agenda. It's a
tidy, presentable deliverable.

## Nuclear pharmacy

**Prerequisite:** most nuclear APPEs require completion of a **nuclear pharmacy certificate
program** first — check with your school early, because this constrains eligibility.

**Where:** commercial radiopharmacies (Cardinal Health Nuclear & Precision Health Solutions,
Jubilant RadioPharma, Curium, PharmaLogic) and hospital nuclear medicine departments.
Growing overlap with **theranostics** in oncology (Lu-177, Ac-225), which makes this less
niche than it used to be.

## Global health & international rotations

Programs vary widely by school. Documented destinations across US pharmacy schools include
**Belize, China, England, Oman, South Africa, Spain, and Thailand**, plus resource-limited
settings through mission and NGO partnerships.

**Things to sort out early:** licensure and scope limitations abroad, malpractice coverage,
visas, immunizations, cost (usually self-funded), and whether your school grants APPE credit.
Ask your OEE — most schools have a defined international APPE policy and a short list of
approved partners.

**Domestic global-health-adjacent alternatives:** CDC global health programs, refugee and
immigrant health clinics, FQHCs, IHS (see [03](03-federal-government.md)), and Ryan White
HIV clinics.

---

## Veterinary pharmacy

Compounding for non-human patients — dramatically different dosing, formulations, and
regulatory framework.

**Where:** veterinary teaching hospitals (Penn Vet in Philadelphia, Cornell in Ithaca, Tufts
Cummings in North Grafton MA, VMRCVM), veterinary compounding pharmacies, zoos and aquariums
with formal veterinary services.

**Related:** the Society of Veterinary Hospital Pharmacists.

---

## Correctional pharmacy

State departments of corrections and the **federal Bureau of Prisons** both employ
pharmacists. Formulary management under tight constraints, chronic disease management, ID
(HIV/HCV) at high prevalence, and psychiatric medication management. BOP participates in
federal student programs — see [03](03-federal-government.md).

---

## Compounding

**Sterile (503A/503B):** hospital IV rooms, outsourcing facilities. USP <797> and <800> in
practice. Directly relevant if you want health-system operations.
**Non-sterile:** independent compounding pharmacies — hormone replacement, dermatology,
pediatric formulations, veterinary.

Related standards work: [USP](https://www.usp.org/services/academic-connection) (see
[05](05-associations-policy-standards.md)) and the Alliance for Pharmacy Compounding.

---

## Hospice & palliative care

Symptom management at end of life, opioid conversion and rotation, deprescribing, and
family-facing counseling. Hospice organizations and palliative care consult services both
host students. Emotionally demanding and consistently rated as formative.

---

## Drug information

Systematic literature search, formal response writing, drug monograph development, and
formulary support. Several academic medical centers and colleges of pharmacy run formal
**drug information centers**; industry equivalents sit in Medical Information departments
(see [04](04-industry-and-regulatory.md)).

The NIH Clinical Center lists **drug information / drug policy** among its APPE tracks.

A drug information APPE is one of the best preparations for an industry medical information
fellowship, and it sharpens writing in a way that shows up everywhere else.

---

## Medical writing & communications

**Settings:** CRO medical writing groups ([Parexel](https://jobs.parexel.com/en/medicalwritingjobs),
[IQVIA](https://www.iqvia.com/library/fact-sheets/medical-and-scientific-writing-solutions)),
pharma medical communications, medical communications agencies, publishers, and CE providers.

**Document types you'd learn:** clinical study protocols, clinical study reports, regulatory
submission sections, patient narratives, safety reports, manuscripts, and continuing-education
content.

**Related:** the American Medical Writers Association (AMWA).

---

## Pharmacy administration & leadership

Budgeting, drug shortage management, staffing models, 340B compliance, supply chain, and P&T
governance. Frequently precepted by a Director of Pharmacy — an excellent letter-writer if
you're interested in health-system leadership.

**Related:** ASHP's [health-system pharmacy administration and leadership resources](https://www.ashp.org/-/media/assets/pharmacy-practice/resource-centers/career-transitions/docs/ASHP-PALRC-HSPAL-Residency-Candidate-Guide-12-2022.pdf)
and HSPAL residency pathways.

---

## Public health & harm reduction

Health department immunization programs, opioid overdose prevention and naloxone
distribution, syringe service programs, tobacco cessation, and emergency preparedness.
State and city health departments across the Northeast run all of these, and pharmacists are
increasingly central to them. Almost always a cold-outreach arrangement.

## Quick comparison

| Elective | Competition | Lead time | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Poison control / toxicology | Moderate | 3–6 mo | EM, critical care, ID interest |
| Informatics | Low | 2–4 mo | Health-system, tech-adjacent careers |
| Medication safety | Low | 2–4 mo | Administration, quality |
| Nuclear | Low (gated by certificate) | 6+ mo | Niche specialization |
| Global health | Varies | 6–12 mo | Public health, mission-driven |
| Veterinary | Low | 3–6 mo | Compounding, genuinely unusual |
| Correctional | Low | 4–6 mo (clearance) | Public health, ID, psych |
| Drug information | Low–moderate | 2–4 mo | Industry, medical information |
| Medical writing | Moderate | 4–6 mo | Industry, regulatory |
| Administration | Low | 2–4 mo | HSPAL, leadership |
