# 03 — Federal & Government APPEs

Back to [index](../README.md).

The most competitive and most distinctive non-clinical rotations in pharmacy. Nearly all
require **U.S. citizenship**, all require long onboarding lead times, and almost all are
unpaid academic credit (the COSTEP externships are the paid exception).

---

## FDA — Pharmacy Student Experiential Program (PSEP)

**Location:** Silver Spring, MD (White Oak campus) and other FDA sites
**Duration:** 4–6 weeks, offered year-round
**Link:** [FDA PSEP overview](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/scientific-internships-fellowships-trainees-and-non-us-citizens/fda-pharmacy-student-experiential-program) ·
[How to apply](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/fda-pharmacy-student-experiential-program/how-apply) ·
[About PSEP](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/fda-pharmacy-student-experiential-program/about-fda-psep) ·
[Information for placed students](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/fda-pharmacy-student-experiential-program/information-placed-students)

> ⛔ **Status alert:** As of the research date, FDA's pages reported that PSEP APPE
> opportunities are **currently unavailable and not accepting applications for the
> 2026–2027 academic year**. Check the "How to Apply" page directly before writing this off
> for later cycles — this has been a pause, not a permanent closure, in the past.

**What you'd do:** Rotations sit across FDA's centers and offices — CDER (drug evaluation),
CBER (biologics), CDRH (devices), the Office of Clinical Pharmacology, drug safety and
pharmacovigilance, regulatory review, and policy offices.

**Eligibility:** U.S. citizenship, enrollment in an ACPE-accredited PharmD program,
eligible to begin APPEs.

**Related FDA entry points if PSEP stays closed:**
- **CDER Student Volunteer Service Program (SVSP)** — PSEP historically sits inside SVSP.
- **ORISE fellowships** — [FDA scientific fellowships and training programs in CDER](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/jobs-center-drug-evaluation-and-research-cder/scientific-fellowships-and-training-programs-cder).
  ORISE support has been available for pre-doctoral students in the Office of Clinical
  Pharmacology. Not an APPE, but the same building and the same network.
- **JRCOSTEP/SRCOSTEP** — FDA is a participating agency (see USPHS below).

## NIH Clinical Center — Pharmacy Department APPE

**Location:** Bethesda, MD
**Reported deadline:** **October 31**
**Link:** [NIH CC clinical electives](https://www.cc.nih.gov/training/students/clinical_electives)

**Rotation types reported:**
- Administration
- Drug information / drug policy
- Pharmacy informatics
- Inpatient pharmacy operations
- Outpatient pharmacy operations
- Pharmacokinetics / clinical research / clinical pharmacology

**Application:** CV plus a letter of intent, submitted with a specific file-naming
convention (the NIH page specifies it — follow it exactly, it's a screening step).
Applications have historically gone to the Clinical Center pharmacy education contact;
**pull the current email off the NIH page rather than reusing one from a forum post.**

**International students:** NIH has historically welcomed them with an explicit caveat —
additional paperwork, longer processing, and a real risk of delay or cancellation. If you
need a visa accommodation, apply early and keep a backup rotation.

**Adjacent NIH options:** the **National Cancer Institute** and other institutes host
research-focused trainees; NIH's IRTA/postbac programs are a separate (non-APPE) pathway
worth knowing about if research is your endgame.

## CDC — Pharmacy Student Experiential Program

**Location:** Atlanta, GA (some rotations elsewhere/remote)
**Reported window:** applications open around **September**, deadline **January 1**
**Link:** [CDC rotations — USPHS Pharmacist PAC](https://dcp.psc.gov/OSG/pharmacy/cdc.aspx)

Run by volunteer pharmacists and staff within CDC. You get a general grounding in CDC's
mission and public health priorities, and see where pharmacists sit inside CDC — antimicrobial
resistance and stewardship, immunization programs, drug overdose prevention, global health,
emergency preparedness, and health policy that touches pharmacy practice.

Strong pairing with an ID or ambulatory care clinical rotation if you're building a public
health narrative.

## Veterans Affairs (VA)

**Locations:** ~170 medical centers nationwide — the largest single employer of pharmacists
in the U.S.
**Duration:** typically 3–7 weeks, full-time
**Link:** each facility's own page under [va.gov](https://www.va.gov/) → *Work with us →
Internships and fellowships*

**Rotation areas commonly available:** acute care, ambulatory care (VA pharmacists have
broad scope — many run their own clinics under scope of practice agreements), geriatrics,
mental health, pain management, pharmacoeconomics, and institutional practice.

**How to apply:** VA is decentralized. There is no national APPE portal. Find the facility,
find its pharmacy education or residency page, and follow the local registration process —
usually coordinated through your school's OEE. Onboarding (background check, badging,
occupational health) is the slow part; start 4–6 months out.

**Why VA is worth targeting:** ambulatory care scope of practice is unusually advanced, the
EHR gives students real access, and a VA APPE is a well-understood credential if you're
aiming at a VA residency later.

**Northeast/Mid-Atlantic VA sites to look at:** see
[09 — Northeast & Mid-Atlantic Sites](09-northeast-mid-atlantic-sites.md).

> Note: **VALOR** (VA Learning Opportunities Residency) is a *nursing* program. It comes up
> constantly in searches for VA student programs and is not a pharmacy pathway.

## Indian Health Service (IHS)

**Locations:** IHS direct-service facilities across ~39 states; heavy presence in the
Southwest (AZ, NM, OK), the Northern Plains, and Alaska
**Link:** [IHS Pharmacy student opportunities](https://www.ihs.gov/pharmacy/studentops/) ·
[IHS student opportunities](https://www.ihs.gov/careeropps/studentopportunities/) ·
[Residencies and rotations](https://www.ihs.gov/careeropps/studentopportunities/residencies-and-rotations/)

IHS has collaborative agreements with universities for its APPE program; students from
**80+ universities in 39 states** complete experiential training at IHS facilities.

**How to apply:** Start with your school's experiential coordinator — confirm your school
has an agreement with your target site. If not, your coordinator (not you) contacts the IHS
Pharmacy Program. A current list of rotation sites and contacts is downloadable from the IHS
Pharmacy site under *IHS Rotations/Clerkships*.

**Terms:** unpaid, for academic credit, final-year students. **You pay travel and housing** —
budget for it, since many sites are genuinely remote.

**Why it's a standout rotation:** IHS pharmacists practice at the top of their license, often
with prescriptive authority and independent clinics. It's one of the few places a student
sees pharmacist-run primary care as the norm.

**Paid IHS options:** JRCOSTEP, SRCOSTEP, and Senior Year Externship Rotations.

## USPHS Commissioned Corps — JRCOSTEP & SRCOSTEP

**Link:** [usphs.gov/students](https://www.usphs.gov/students/) ·
[JRCOSTEP FAQs (PDF)](https://www.usphs.gov/media/yunnlaiu/jrcostep-faqs.pdf)

These are **paid** externships, not APPEs — but they're the most direct route into federal
pharmacy and they feed the same networks.

| | JRCOSTEP | SRCOSTEP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | Students with ≥1 year of school remaining (P2/P3) | Students in their final year |
| Length | 31–120 days, during school breaks | Longer commitment |
| Reported window | **July 1 – September 15** | Typically winter/spring |
| Pay | Yes — active duty pay/allowances | Yes, with a service obligation |

**Participating agencies:** FDA, IHS, CMS, CDC, National Park Service, and the Program
Support Center. That list is itself a useful map of where federal pharmacists work.

**Eligibility:** U.S. citizenship, age and physical-fitness standards, good academic standing.

## Practical notes for every federal application

- **Citizenship is usually non-negotiable.** Confirm before investing effort.
- **Onboarding is the bottleneck.** Background investigation, fingerprinting, and badging
  routinely take 2–4 months *after* acceptance. A late acceptance can still fail on logistics.
- **Affiliation agreements with federal agencies are slow.** Ask your OEE in September, not
  in March.
- **Unpaid + expensive location.** Bethesda, Silver Spring, and DC are costly. Ask your school
  about experiential travel stipends; several exist and go unclaimed.
- **Apply to more than one.** FDA, NIH, and CDC each take a small cohort. Treat them as
  lottery tickets you buy in bulk, with a solid non-federal backup behind them.
