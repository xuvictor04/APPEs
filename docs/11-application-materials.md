What competitive APPE programs ask for, and how to make each piece work.

## The standard packet

Nearly every program in this repo wants some subset of:

| Item | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **CV** | 2–4 pages is normal for a P3. Not a resume. |
| **Letter of intent** | One page. Program-specific. The piece that actually decides it. |
| **Essay** | AMCP asks for 500 words on career goals and interest in managed care |
| **Letters of recommendation** | ACCP wants **two from faculty**. Ask 3–4 weeks out. |
| **OEE letter** | ACCP explicitly requires one confirming scheduling and the affiliation agreement |
| **Transcript** | Sometimes unofficial is fine |
| **Application form** | Often a web form with a hard character limit — draft in a doc first |
| **File naming convention** | NIH specifies one. Follow it exactly; it's a screening step. |

## CV

**Structure** (reverse chronological within each section):

```
Name, credentials, contact
Education
Licensure & Certifications        (intern license, BLS/ACLS, immunization certificate)
Pharmacy Experience               (IPPEs, internships, tech work)
Research Experience
Teaching Experience
Leadership & Professional Service (chapter offices, committees)
Presentations                     (posters, platform, CE)
Publications
Honors & Awards
Professional Memberships          (ASHP, APhA, AMCP, ACCP, IPhO…)
Community Service
```

**What actually helps:**
- **List your IPPE and APPE rotations** with site, preceptor, and dates. Programs want to see
  where you've been.
- **Quantify.** "Provided 120+ immunizations" beats "administered vaccines."
- **Show outcomes, not duties.** "Implemented a refill reminder workflow that reduced missed
  pickups" beats "assisted with refills."
- **Keep it current.** Update after every rotation, not once a year. This is the single
  highest-return habit in the P4 year.
- **Consistent formatting matters more than you'd think** — it's the first proxy for
  attention to detail a reviewer sees.

**Reference:** [Crafting Your CV — a residency program's perspective](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/crafting-your-cv-a-pharmacy-residency-programs-perspective) ·
[Pharmacy residency CV tips](https://confidentcareermoves.com/pharmacy-residency-curriculum-vitae-help/)

## Letter of intent

The differentiator. Most applicants submit a competent CV; most letters of intent are generic.

**Structure** (one page, four paragraphs):

**1. The hook and thesis.** Name the exact program you're applying to, how you heard about it,
and why you want it. One or two sentences of genuine specificity beats a paragraph of warmth.

**2–3. Background and qualifications.** Evidence you're a fit. The most common failure is
listing experiences — which just makes the letter a prose CV. Instead, **choose one strong
example and explain why it mattered**: a patient interaction, a recommendation you made and
what happened, a project that changed what you want to do.

**4. The close.** What you'll gain, what you'll contribute, and how this connects to your
stated career direction. Thank them; state your availability.

**Rules:**
- **Name the organization and something specific about it.** If the letter would work with
  the name swapped out, it isn't finished.
- **Name a function, not a field.** "I want to build toward regulatory affairs" lands.
  "I'm interested in industry" doesn't.
- **One page. Always.** BMS explicitly asks for one page.
- **Proofread out loud.** Then have someone else read it.

**Reference:** [Letter of Intent Tips (Univ. of Wisconsin, PDF)](https://students.pharmacy.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/Letter-of-Intent-Tips.pdf) ·
[6th Year Perspective: Guide for Application-Based APPEs](https://rhochistj.org/RhoChiPost/6th-year-perspective-guide-for-application-based-appes/)

Skeleton: [templates/letter-of-intent.md](../templates/letter-of-intent.md)

## Letters of recommendation

- **Ask 3–4 weeks ahead.** Minimum.
- **Ask people who can speak to specifics.** A faculty member who supervised a project beats
  a bigger name who taught you in a 200-person lecture.
- **Send one email covering all your applications** — list every program, its deadline, and
  its submission method in a single table. Your writers will thank you and you'll get better
  letters.
- **Include a packet:** your CV, your letter of intent draft, and 3–4 bullets on what you'd
  like them to emphasize.
- **Follow up once**, a week before the deadline. Then thank them, and tell them the outcome.

## Cold outreach to a preceptor

How most non-advertised rotations actually happen. Full template:
[templates/outreach-email.md](../templates/outreach-email.md)

**The rules that make it work:**
1. **Email one specific person**, not a department inbox. Find the board-certified specialist
   or the residency program director by name.
2. **Subject line does the work:** `PharmD student — APPE rotation inquiry, [Month Year]`
3. **Under 200 words.** Who you are, why *them* specifically, what dates, what you'd bring.
4. **Attach your CV.** One PDF, named `Lastname_Firstname_CV.pdf`.
5. **Remove the friction.** Say your OEE handles the affiliation agreement, and offer to
   connect them directly. Offer the ASHP
   [rotation templates](https://www.ashp.org/pharmacy-practice/resource-centers/preceptor-toolkit)
   if they've never precepted.
6. **Follow up once after 10–14 days.** Then move on.
7. **Send 10, expect 2–3 replies.** This is a numbers game and non-responses are not personal.

## The outreach package for school-arranged APPEs ✅

Verbatim guidance from the July 2026 IPhO review, which is the standard for the dozen-plus
companies listed as "school-arranged" in [04](04-industry-and-regulatory.md):

- **Ask the experiential office first** whether the school already has a contract, an approved
  preceptor, or an alumni contact at the company.
- **Prepare a concise email** with: your school, PharmD graduation year, **exact APPE block
  dates**, functional interests, and **the experiential-office contact who handles contracts**.
- **Attach a current CV and a tailored one-page letter of interest.** ⚠️ **Do not send
  transcripts, background information, or sensitive personal data until requested through an
  official process.**
- **Confirm before committing:** modality, location, academic credit, insurance,
  background-check requirements, expenses, and contract completion.

Two of those deserve emphasis. **Naming your contract contact in the first email** removes the
company's biggest unknown — who at your school does the paperwork. And **withholding sensitive
documents until an official request** protects you: a legitimate program will ask through a
formal channel, and nothing about an initial inquiry requires a transcript or a date of birth.

One more, from the same source, aimed at third-party virtual rotation vendors: **confirm
academic credit, preceptor qualifications, affiliation terms, schedule, and any student or
school costs before enrolling.** If a program charges you, that is a reason to slow down and
check with your OEE.

## Interviews

Some programs interview (industry and federal especially).

**Expect:**
- "Why this organization, and why this function?"
- "Walk me through your CV."
- "Tell me about a time you handled conflict / failure / a difficult patient."
- "What do you want to do after graduation?" — have an answer, even a provisional one
- A clinical or drug information question, sometimes
- "What questions do you have for us?" — always have three

**Prepare:**
- Research the organization's actual work. For BMS, know a pipeline product. For AMCP, know
  what the AMCP Format is. For PQA, know what a Star Rating measure is.
- Have 3–4 **STAR stories** ready (Situation, Task, Action, Result) that you can adapt.
- Practice out loud. Recording yourself once is unpleasant and very effective.
- Send a thank-you email within 24 hours.

## Common failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Found the program after the deadline | Work the calendar in [02](02-timeline-and-deadlines.md) monthly |
| Accepted, but no affiliation agreement in time | Talk to your OEE **before** applying |
| Generic letter of intent | Name the organization and one specific thing about its work |
| Asked for letters too late | 3–4 weeks minimum, with a packet |
| Applied to one dream program only | Apply to six; expect one or two |
| Ignored the file naming convention | Follow it exactly — it's a deliberate screen |
| Missed a federal onboarding window | Budget 3–6 months after acceptance |
| Didn't confirm details before day one | Email the site two weeks out |

## During the rotation

Getting the rotation is half of it; the other half is what you take out of it.

- **Ask for the syllabus and expectations a week before you start.**
- **Take initiative.** Ask to see things, ask for career advice, ask for more. Preceptors
  recommend the students who engage — and residency directors weight
  letters of intent, letters of recommendation, and APPE performance above almost everything else.
- **Keep an intervention log.** Every recommendation you make, and what happened. You'll need
  concrete examples in December and you won't remember them.
- **Ask about a deliverable in week one** — a poster abstract, a monograph, a protocol —
  and finish it before you leave.
- **Ask for the letter of recommendation while you're still there**, not three months later.

**Reference:** [Tips for APPE/IPPE Pharmacy Rotations](https://gdpharmd.com/rotationtips/) ·
[Preparing for APPEs](https://u.osu.edu/pharmdambassadors/2018/11/13/preparing-for-appes/)
