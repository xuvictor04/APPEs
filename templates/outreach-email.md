# Cold Outreach Email — Template

For preceptors and sites without a formal application. Under 200 words. One specific person.

---

**Subject:** PharmD student — APPE rotation inquiry, [Month Year]

Dear Dr. [Last Name],

My name is [Name], and I am a [P3] PharmD candidate at [School], graduating in [Year]. I am
scheduling my APPE rotations for [academic year] and am writing to ask whether you would
consider precepting a student.

I am particularly interested in [specific area], and [one sentence on why *this* preceptor or
site — their service, their published work, a specific program they run]. [One sentence of
relevant background: an IPPE, an internship, a project, a certification.]

I am available for the following blocks: [dates]. My school's Office of Experiential
Education handles the affiliation agreement and all required documentation, and I am happy to
connect you with them directly. If it would help, ASHP publishes ready-made rotation
descriptions and evaluation templates I can send along.

My CV is attached. Thank you for your time — I would appreciate the chance to speak with you,
and I am glad to be pointed toward a colleague if this isn't the right fit.

Best regards,
[Name], PharmD Candidate [Year]
[School] · [Email] · [Phone]

---

## Rules

1. **One named person.** Not `pharmacy@hospital.org`. Find the specialist, the residency
   program director, or the clinical coordinator by name.
2. **Under 200 words.** Length reads as a burden.
3. **Attach one PDF**, named `Lastname_Firstname_CV.pdf`.
4. **Remove friction** — say your OEE handles the paperwork, and offer the ASHP templates.
   Most declines are about effort, not about you.
5. **Follow up once** after 10–14 days, then stop.
6. **Send ten.** Expect two or three replies. That's a normal, successful rate.
7. **Track every send** in the tracker so you don't double-email a department.

## Variant: warm intro through a faculty member

> Dr. [Faculty], you mentioned you know [Name] at [Site]. I'm scheduling APPEs for [year]
> and hoping to do a rotation in [area]. Would you be willing to introduce us, or let me
> mention your name when I reach out?

Warm intros convert far better than cold ones. Ask your faculty who they know before you
start emailing strangers.
